01 — The story

I'm based in the United States and recently finished a Master's in Computer Science. The CS degree taught me one thing more than anything else: I'd rather build than research. The hours I lost track of were always the ones spent making something real that someone could use.

Somewhere along the way I noticed a pattern in my own GitHub. HR management. Shift scheduling. Time tracking. A resume builder. A corporate site for a real company. Five projects, all clustered around one space — workforce and HR. That wasn't a plan. It was a niche I'd been quietly developing for two years without naming it.

So I named it. I want to build software for the people who manage people — small teams, mid-sized startups, the kind of companies that ship every week and feel every bug. I use modern tooling and AI as force multipliers. I read fast, adapt fast, and don't need hand-holding. The next role is somewhere in HR-tech, workforce SaaS, or vertical B2B — and I'm ready to start.

02 — What I'm good at

The things you can actually hire me for.

  1. /01

    Shipping a Next.js + TypeScript app to production.

    From `npx create-next-app` to a public URL with auth, a database, payments and a CI pipeline — in days, not months. I've done it five times.

  2. /02

    Designing dashboards that don't drown the user.

    HR, scheduling, and internal-tool UIs where information density matters. I obsess over the at-a-glance read.

  3. /03

    Reading messy code and finding the seam.

    New codebase, weird conventions, half-documented patterns — I diff, trace, and ship a fix that looks like the rest of the file.

  4. /04

    Using AI tooling like a professional.

    Claude, Cursor, and Copilot compress my output 3–10× without compromising review-readiness. I treat prompts like commits.

  5. /05

    Translating non-technical pain into features.

    Talking to a manager about a Sunday-night roster problem and turning that conversation into a screen they actually use.

  6. /06

    Owning a project end-to-end, alone.

    Schema, API, UI, copy, deploy. I'm not waiting for someone else to start. The five projects on my work page are all solo builds.

03 — What I'm not good at (yet)

Things I'm still learning. No fluff.

  1. /01

    Performance engineering at scale.

    I can build for 100 users. I haven't optimized for 100,000. Profiling, caching strategies, query plans — I know the words; I haven't lived inside them yet.

  2. /02

    Production incident response.

    My code has been in production. I haven't been on-call at 3 a.m. I'd rather be honest than pretend I have the scars.

  3. /03

    Mentoring junior engineers.

    I learn by reading other people's code. I haven't yet been the one writing code others learn from. That's a job I want.

  4. /04

    Saying no to scope creep.

    I get excited and overbuild. I'm working on cutting 20% out of every spec before I touch the keyboard.
